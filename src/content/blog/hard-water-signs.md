# 5 Signs You Have Hard Water in Your Raleigh Home

If you're like most Raleigh homeowners, you might not realize that hard water is affecting your daily life. Raleigh's water supply contains moderate to high levels of calcium and magnesium, making it "hard" water that can cause problems throughout your home.

Here are the five most common signs of hard water in Triangle homes:

## 1. Spotty Dishes and Glassware

Are your glasses coming out of the dishwasher with white spots or a filmy residue? This is one of the most visible signs of hard water. The mineral deposits left behind after water evaporates create those annoying spots that never seem to wash off completely.

**The fix:** A water softener removes these minerals before they reach your dishwasher.

## 2. Dry Skin and Hair

Hard water doesn't rinse soap away as effectively, leaving a residue on your skin and hair. If you've noticed your skin feeling tight and dry after showering, or your hair looking dull and lifeless, hard water could be the culprit.

**Raleigh homeowners report:** Softer skin and more manageable hair within days of installing a water softener.

## 3. Scale Buildup on Fixtures

Take a look at your showerhead and faucets. Do you see white, chalky buildup around the openings? This is limescale - mineral deposits from hard water that can eventually clog pipes and reduce water pressure.

## 4. Appliance Issues

Your water heater, dishwasher, and washing machine all suffer from hard water. The minerals create scale buildup that:
- Reduces efficiency
- Increases energy costs
- Shortens appliance lifespan by 30% or more

## 5. Increased Soap Usage

Hard water prevents soap from lathering properly. If you find yourself using more shampoo, dish soap, and laundry detergent than you think you should, hard water is likely to blame. Most homeowners use 50% less soap after installing a water softener.

Raleigh's municipal water averages **7-10 grains per gallon** of hardness. While not the hardest in the country, it's enough to cause the issues listed above over time.

## Solution: Whole House Water Softener

A water softener removes calcium and magnesium through ion exchange, replacing them with sodium or potassium. The result?
